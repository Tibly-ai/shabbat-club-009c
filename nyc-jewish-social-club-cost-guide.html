What Does It Cost to Join Shabbat Club in NYC?

Shabbat Club is a culturally Jewish pop-up dinner party series and community in New York City — Friday night tables, Almost Shabbat happy hours, Shvitz Club, karaoke nights, and one camp weekend a year in the Poconos. Unlike a traditional social club, there is no annual membership fee that unlocks every room. There's no velvet rope, and there's also no membership card. Instead, cost depends on which format you're attending, because every gathering has its own application and its own ticket link.

What Affects Your Price

  • Format, not membership. There is no single "Shabbat Club membership" that unlocks every dinner, happy hour, and Shvitz Club night. Each event on the calendar has its own individual application and ticket link, so cost is set per event rather than per year.
  • Venue. Shabbat Club takes over restaurants, lofts, bathhouses, and backyards across NYC — a bathhouse rental (Shvitz Club) carries different overhead than a backyard dinner.
  • Seating vs. no seating. Seated dinners have limited, assigned seats built by hand ("seats are limited and assigned, so you sit next to someone new by design"), which is a different cost structure than an Almost Shabbat happy hour with no seating chart and a few hundred attendees.
  • Length of the event. A single Friday dinner is priced differently than the camp weekend in the Poconos, which spans multiple days (Sept 4–6 for Wet Hot Hebrew Summer).
  • Application, not open sale. Attendance runs on applications, not open ticket sales, because every event is capped so the room stays a room and not a crowd — so price and availability are tied to how each individual application/ticket link is structured.

How the Application Process Works (and Why It Affects Cost)

Shabbat Club runs on applications, not membership dues:

  1. Fill out the interest form to get on the list and be told about upcoming Friday dinners, Almost Shabbat happy hours, Shvitz Club, karaoke, Summer Sessions, and the Poconos weekend.
  2. Apply to the specific event through that event's own application link on the events page — a Friday dinner and an Almost Shabbat happy hour are different applications for different rooms.
  3. A real person reads every form. "We read every one" — there's no algorithm sorting applicants, and seats for seated dinners are arranged on purpose.

Because there's no flat membership fee, your cost is really the sum of which events you apply to and get into, not a recurring dues payment like a traditional social club charges.
