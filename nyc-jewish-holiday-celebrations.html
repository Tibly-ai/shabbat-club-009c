Jewish Holiday Celebrations in NYC with Shabbat Club

Fridays are for Friends — and so are Purim, Passover, Hanukkah, and Rosh Hashanah. Shabbat Club is a culturally Jewish pop-up dinner party series and community in New York City, and the holidays are where the club gets loudest. We take over restaurants, lofts, and backyards across NYC to run the same kind of night we run every Friday: candles, a blessing, a room full of people who mostly didn't know each other at 7pm — just built around a different date on the calendar.

The Jewish part of Shabbat Club is cultural rather than religious. You'll meet founders, nurses, comedians, teachers, and people three weeks into a new city, and nobody sits with the friends they arrived with for very long. Every event is capped so the room stays a room and not a crowd, which is why attendance runs on applications rather than open ticket sales.

How to Get a Seat at a Holiday Event

Shabbat Club runs on applications, not open ticket sales, because every event is capped so the room stays a room and not a crowd. Here's how it works for holiday nights specifically:

  1. Check the calendar. Holiday celebrations are listed on the events page alongside Friday dinners, Almost Shabbat happy hours, Shvitz Club, karaoke nights, and the camp weekend in the Poconos.
  2. Apply through that event's own link. Each holiday gathering — Purim, the Passover Seder, Hanukkah candle lighting, Rosh Hashanah — has its own individual application, separate from a regular Friday dinner.
  3. A real person reads every form. There's no algorithm sorting people out. "We read every one."
  4. Seats are limited and assigned. For seated formats like a Seder, seating is arranged on purpose so you sit next to someone new by design.

If you'd rather get on the list first and be pointed to the right link when a holiday event opens up, fill out the interest form — that's how Shabbat Club knows to send you the application for the next Purim, Seder, Hanukkah, or Rosh Hashanah night.
