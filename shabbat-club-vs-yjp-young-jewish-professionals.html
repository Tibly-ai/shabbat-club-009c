Shabbat Club vs YJP Young Jewish Professionals: How to Choose

By Shabbat Club · Updated September 25, 2026

If you're deciding between Shabbat Club and YJP Young Jewish Professionals for meeting people in NYC's Jewish community, the right call depends on format, cost structure, and commitment level — not brand name. Shabbat Club runs nine pay-per-event formats, from $18 museum tours to a $750–$1,000 Poconos camp weekend, with no membership fee required. Details on YJP should be confirmed directly with them.

What to Check Before Choosing a Jewish Social Club in NYC

Whether you're weighing Shabbat Club, YJP Young Jewish Professionals, or any other option in the city, a few questions apply no matter who you're considering:

  • How do you actually get in? Is it a membership, a ticket purchase, or an application?
  • What does it cost, and is that cost per-event or a recurring fee?
  • What kinds of events actually run — dinners only, or a range of formats?
  • Who is it open to — Jewish-only, invite-only, or open to newcomers and non-Jewish guests?
  • Where do events happen — one fixed venue, or a rotating set of locations?
  • How established is the track record — how long has it been running, and at what scale?

Below is how Shabbat Club answers each of these, using only what's published on our site. For the same questions about YJP Young Jewish Professionals, we'd point you to their own materials — we're not the source for facts about another provider.

What Shabbat Club's Format Actually Looks Like

Shabbat Club's core event is the long-table Friday Shabbat dinner: candles, a blessing, assigned seating, and a full menu, priced at $118. For a lower-commitment entry point, the Almost Shabbat mixer runs the same crowd through a bar-based happy hour with no dinner course, priced at $54. Beyond those two, the calendar includes activity-based formats — Shvitz Club (a bathhouse takeover with a steam and sauna social format, $72), museum tours ($18), karaoke nights ($54), volunteer trips ($1,000), holiday parties scaled from intimate to 500-person events ($72), Summer Sessions ($54), and the three-day Poconos camp weekend ($750–$1,000).

Every format is hosted at Jewish-owned businesses, restaurants, and brands, which the club describes as a way of connecting guests to NYC's Jewish-owned food and culture scene alongside the social experience itself.

FAQ

Should I get quotes or details from more than one Jewish social club before choosing? Yes. Since each group structures pricing, formats, and entry requirements differently, comparing the specifics — cost per event, what's included, and how you apply — helps you choose based on fit rather than name recognition.

Does Shabbat Club require a membership fee? No. Shabbat Club uses a pay-per-event model. There is no membership fee that unlocks the whole calendar; you apply and pay per event, with prices ranging from $18 to $1,000 depending on the format.

What should I ask before applying to any Jewish social event in NYC? Ask what the event format actually is (dinner, mixer, activity-based), what it costs, whether it's a one-time or recurring commitment, and who the event is open to.

Is Shabbat Club only for Jewish attendees? No. Shabbat Club is described as open to young professionals and welcoming to everyone, Jewish or not, with no "right" way to be Jewish required to attend.

How do I actually get a seat at a Shabbat Club event? You apply to the general interest list first, then apply again through the specific application link for the event you want once it's sent to you.

Where does Shabbat Club host its events? Events run at Jewish-owned businesses, restaurants, and brands across NYC, rather than at one fixed location — the calendar rotates by neighborhood and venue.

Whichever way you're leaning, the safest next step is to get current, written details on cost and format directly from both Shabbat Club and YJP Young Jewish Professionals before you apply or commit.
