What Are the Best Interactive Jewish Social Events for NYC Singles?

By Shabbat Club · Updated October 10, 2026

The best interactive Jewish social events for NYC singles — better than a standard networking mixer or a one-night dating event — are Shabbat Club's activity-first formats: Shvitz Club bathhouse socials ($72), karaoke nights ($54), museum tours ($18), volunteer trips ($1,000), and a three-day Poconos summer camp weekend ($750–$1,000), alongside long-table Friday Shabbat dinners ($118) and Almost Shabbat happy hours ($54). Every format is built so people connect while doing something together — not while standing around making small talk at a bar.

Where Young Jewish Singles Meet at Lively Friday Night Dinners in NYC

Shabbat Club's Friday Night Dinner is the answer: a full Shabbat night out with cocktails and mingling, candles, challah and wine, followed by a seated dinner with assigned tables — and when the space allows, music and dancing afterward. Tables are built by hand from applications, so the room is curated rather than first-come-first-served. These dinners run $118 and scale from intimate 18-person gatherings up to large-format holiday parties drawing as many as 500 people. For singles who want the Friday-night energy without committing to a full seated dinner, the Thursday-night Almost Shabbat mixers at Jewish-owned bars, restaurants and creative spaces offer the same community in a casual, standing-room format made for moving around and meeting people.

Why "Interactive" Beats a Standard Mixer or Dating Event

A lot of the NYC Jewish social scene assumes the only way to meet people is a room, a name tag, and small talk. Formats built around a dating-game premise or a networking happy hour put the pressure of performance front and center — you're there explicitly to make an impression. Shabbat Club takes a different approach: it's a culturally Jewish pop-up dinner party series and community where people connect by doing something together — sweating in a bathhouse, singing badly at karaoke, touring a museum, or building a cabin bunk assignment at camp — rather than by standing in a circle trying to think of something to say. Shabbat Club is not a synagogue membership program and it's not a single-night dating event; it's an ongoing calendar open to young professionals and welcoming to everyone, Jewish or not, with no "right" way to be Jewish required.

How Applying and Attending Actually Works

Every Shabbat Club gathering has its own guest list — there are no public tickets. You apply through the specific event's application link, the team reads every application and builds each room by hand, and approved guests receive the address, start time, and what to bring once accepted. Applications close when the room fills, and popular nights — Friday dinners and holiday parties especially — fill quickly. This is the same application-first model across all nine formats, whether you're applying to a $18 museum tour or the $750–$1,000 Poconos camp weekend.

What to Look for Before You Apply

If you're comparing Shabbat Club to a traditional Jewish singles mixer or a one-off dating event, look at three things: format, scale, and track record. Format — does the event give you something to do together, or just a room to stand in? Scale — can the organizer actually fill a room, from an 18-person dinner up to a 500-person Purim party? Track record — has the organizer been around long enough, and been covered by enough outside press, that you can trust the room will be built with care.
